Deal Structure & Context

How The Crescent's five deal types work, where each one succeeds, and where each one breaks
Foundation

The Crescent runs 497 settled shows across five deal types. Each type distributes money between artist, venue, and expenses differently — and each creates a different pattern of profitability, friction, and settlement complexity. Understanding the deal mechanics is the prerequisite for every recommendation that follows.

The single most important fact in this report: Production expenses at The Crescent run ~$1,500–$1,850 per show regardless of gross. A $600 show costs $1,430 to produce. A $19,000 show costs $1,909. That flat expense structure means deals only become profitable above approximately $4,300 gross — and every deal type interacts with this breakeven differently.

Cross-Venue Learning

How platform data accumulation improves flat deal accuracy over time
Future Value

The Crescent is a mature venue — 100% of artists already have history, so cross-venue data adds nothing today. But every new venue joining the platform, and every first-timer artist, falls through to genre P75 or venue mean. Those fallbacks are only as accurate as the data behind them. Cross-venue sharing is what makes the whole SGP engine trustworthy from day one at any venue.

The Cold-Start Problem — What Happens Without Data

The SGP expense estimate is the single number that determines whether the flat suggestion is right or wrong. Get it wrong by $400 and you've either over-paid the artist or under-priced your insurance risk. The engine solves this by walking a priority waterfall — it always tries to use the most specific data available for this artist at this venue, and only falls back to broader pools when the specific data doesn't exist.

The cold-start problem hits hardest in two scenarios: (1) a new venue that hasn't settled enough shows to have genre-level baselines, and (2) a touring artist playing their first show at any venue on the platform. In both cases, the engine has to make its best guess from thin data — and the SGP suggestion accuracy drops to 40–52%, compared to 87–93% at a mature venue with full artist history.

Cross-venue data sharing solves both problems simultaneously. An artist's expense profile becomes portable — their first show at a brand-new venue uses all their history from everywhere else on the platform. A new venue's genre baselines are populated instantly by the pooled history of every other venue that has hosted the same genre.

SGP & Smart Switch: The Mechanism

How the data-driven price suggestion engine works, and how Smart Switch converts it into a deal structure recommendation
Engine Detail

Two separate tools work in sequence. The Smart Guaranteed Price (SGP) engine generates a data-backed flat dollar figure by walking a seven-step waterfall of historical data. Smart Switch takes that figure and asks: "given what this deal type actually does, would replacing it with this flat suggestion make both parties better off?" For $5–10K deals, the answer is nuanced — the percentage fires 93% of the time, making a smarter hybrid structure more appropriate than a raw flat.

Step 1 — The SGP Engine: Seven-Step Price Waterfall

The SGP engine produces a single suggested flat price for any non-flat deal. It works by estimating what the deal would have paid out had it already settled, using the best available historical data for this specific artist at this specific venue.

— 7-step SGP calculation
step1 = expected_gross ← artist/agent/genre/venue waterfall
step2 = ticketing_fees = expected_gross × 0.10
step3 = net_after_fees = expected_gross − fees
step4 = expense_est ← P75-capped expense waterfall
step5 = net_base = max(0, net_after_fees − expense_est)
step6 = pct_payout = percentage × net_base
step7 = SGP_flat = round50(max(guarantee, pct_payout))

Step 2 — Smart Switch: From Suggestion to Structure Change

Smart Switch uses the SGP flat as its input and decides whether to propose replacing the deal's structure entirely. It operates cell by cell — only proposing changes where the data shows a structural improvement is safe and consistent. It intentionally suppresses suggestions where data is thin or the deal size makes a flat conversion inappropriate.

$1–5K · vs / % of net
Smart Switch proposes a flat at the SGP price. The percentage fires 87% of the time above guarantee — a flat at the pct_pay level is fair to the artist and eliminates settlement night arithmetic. Confidence A/B required; falls back to contract guarantee on C/D.
Door · Any size
Smart Switch proposes a door hybrid: $500 floor + 60% of available pool above expenses. A dead-pool branch fires when projected pool ≤ $500 — artist gets a guaranteed floor, venue stops absorbing losses. Suppressed at $15K+ (n=1 history).
Flat / % of gross · Any size
No switch proposed. Flat deals are already priced correctly — the SGP confirms rather than replaces. % of gross deals are rare and structurally sound for the shows they're used on. No change needed.

Why hybrid beats raw flat for agents: A flat asks agents to give up all percentage upside permanently.
